About me

I am a graduate student in Industrial & Management Systems Engineering and a graduate research assistant in SIGNS Lab at Montana State University in Bozeman, Montana. My interdisciplinary education combines industrial engineering, nanotechnology and materials science, and international business management.

My principal research direction is the application of machine learning to semiconductor supply chain problems. I am interested in using data-driven models to study supplier concentration, critical minerals and materials, supply disruptions, and resilient engineering decisions. Statistical analysis, systems simulation, and optimization complement this machine-learning-centered approach.

I also explore related applications in smart and advanced manufacturing, manufacturing reliability and maintenance, quality improvement, additive manufacturing, and intelligent transportation systems, including eVTOL-enabled logistics and healthcare mobility. Agentic AI is an additional developing methodological interest.

Research priorities

1. Machine learning for semiconductor supply chains

My primary focus: supply-chain resilience, critical materials, supplier dependencies, and risk-informed decisions.

2. AI and decision-support methods

Applied machine learning, emerging agentic AI, data analytics, simulation, and optimization.

3. Smart manufacturing and reliability

Predictive maintenance, manufacturing quality improvement, process reliability, and additive manufacturing.

4. Intelligent transportation and eVTOL

Data-driven logistics, healthcare transportation, smart cities, and emerging air mobility.
